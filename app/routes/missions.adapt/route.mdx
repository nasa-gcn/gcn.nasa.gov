**Launch Date:** December 10, 2026

**End of Operations:** Mission duration of 1 month

The Antarctic Demonstrator for the Advanced Particle-astrophysics Telescope (ADAPT) is a NASA suborbital mission designed for a high-altitude balloon flight from Antarctica. The 0.5 m x 0.5 m cross-section instrument comprises a scintillating fiber tracker, an imaging CsI calorimeter, and a silicon strip detector for gamma-ray and cosmic-ray measurements. ADAPT will demonstrate key detector technologies for the larger space-based Advanced Particle-astrophysics Telescope (APT) mission and survey the sky for gamma-ray transients in the MeV -GeV energy range during its approximately 30-day flight. It is expected to provide prompt, degree-scale localizations and polarization measurements of several gamma-ray bursts (GRBs), with improved instantaneous sensitivity over existing instruments at energies below 100 MeV. ADAPT will also demonstrate the potential of APT for measuring the elemental abundances of rare ultra-heavy cosmic rays.

The ADAPT instrument is being developed by the APT collaboration, involving Washington University (the PI institution), the Erlangen Centre for Astroparticle Physics, NASA Goddard Space Flight Center, Louisiana State University, INFN Bari, INFN Pisa, the Naval Research Laboratory, the University of Hawaii, and the University of Minnesota.
